Notice the fourth bar is longer than the second. FlashAttention moves more Q, K and V traffic than vanilla does, because tiling forces it to re-read K and V once for every block of Q. It wins anyway, because it deleted the first bar entirely.

Why the win looks small here. At four tokens the score matrix has 16 entries while Q, K and V have 8 each — comparable. Real attention runs at thousands of tokens, where the score matrix is tens of millions of entries and Q, K and V are still only thousands. The first bar grows quadratically; the others do not. That is the entire argument, and this toy badly understates it.

On the loop order. This animation runs the outer loop over blocks of Q and the inner loop over blocks of K and V, which is FlashAttention 2's arrangement and what runs today. The video describes version 1 at 10:52, which had it the other way round — and in that version the output accumulator has to be written back to HBM and read again on every outer sweep. Swapping the loops so the accumulator never leaves SRAM was one of version 2's changes.
